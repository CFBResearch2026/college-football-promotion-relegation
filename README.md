# College Football Promotion & Relegation Research

**An independent, evolving research project exploring whether promotion and relegation could work in American college football.**

What if college football programs could move between competitive tiers based on results on the field—while preserving a financially sustainable system for the institutions involved?

This repository documents proposed rules, conference alignments, historical football data, model versions, and audits used to examine that question. It began with a college-football dynasty adaptation, but the broader research asks how a real-world structure might function.

> **Independent proposal — not NCAA policy.** This is not an NCAA, conference, university, or EA Sports initiative. Alignments and rankings are hypothetical research outputs, not official classifications or approved changes.

## What the project examines

1. **Competitive merit:** Could teams earn promotion or face relegation through transparent sporting results?
2. **Conference structure:** How might tiers be organized while accounting for competitive balance, geography, rivalries, and scheduling?
3. **Historical evidence:** What do game results, classifications, team-name crosswalks, and strength-of-schedule calculations indicate?
4. **Financial feasibility:** What revenue protections, transition arrangements, and media-rights economics would a real-world implementation require?
5. **Implementation:** What rules, governance, and contractual barriers would have to be addressed before any system could be adopted?

One current FBS financial design exercise considers **137 schools across ten proposed conferences/levels**. That design is a scenario for evaluation, not an assertion that the current NCAA structure has changed or that a particular revenue pool has been secured.

## How to interpret the files

The repository contains **proposals**, **source-data compilations**, **derived models**, and **audits**. These are different kinds of evidence:

| Material | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed rules and alignments | Hypothetical choices for debate and testing |
| Historical records and crosswalks | Research inputs that require season- and file-specific source verification |
| Ratings, SOS, and recalculated conferences | Derived outputs dependent on underlying data and model assumptions |
| Audits and release-gate files | Documentation of checks, unresolved gaps, and provisional results |
| Financial and media scenarios | Illustrative modeling, **not** verified future broadcast offers or guaranteed institutional distributions |

**Important data limitations:** Research spans 2014–2026, and the 2026 season is incomplete. Some historical file-level provenance, source reconciliation, and redistribution permissions remain unverified. Cross-division comparisons and model calibration are provisional. See [Sources and Citations](SOURCES_AND_CITATIONS.md) before citing or reusing any data.

## Current status

**Active research / provisional release.** The project is being refined as data are checked and assumptions are challenged. The proposed conference structure should not be mistaken for a finished or endorsed plan. Financial sustainability and media-rights valuation remain major questions under investigation.

## How you can help

Thoughtful criticism is welcome—especially from college football fans, statisticians, historians, athletic-department professionals, and researchers.

- **Found a factual error?** [Open an issue](../../issues) with the filename, season, team, original source link, and suggested correction.
- **Have a rules or alignment suggestion?** [Open an issue](../../issues) explaining the proposed change and why it improves the model.
- **Want to contribute a corrected file or documentation?** Submit a [pull request](../../pulls), identifying the sources and transformations used.

Useful questions include: Are the promotion/relegation criteria fair? Do the proposed levels preserve competitive integrity? Which historic classifications or results need correction? What financial guarantees would schools need? Could the system create enough additional media value to work?

## Research standards

The goal is to distinguish **verified observations**, **assumptions**, and **hypothetical outcomes**. Source listings are not proof that each repository file came from the listed publisher. Please cite exact original records where possible, and flag uncertainty rather than treating estimates as established facts.
